They set off early in the morning, the air still cool and the Thames reflecting the clear sky above. Tom, ever the planner, had insisted on a checklist that they all agreed upon the night before. However, as they unpacked the gear from the car and into the boat, disagreements started to simmer. 

“Absolutely no way we’re leaving without my portable charger,” Jerry insisted, clenching the device as if it were a lifeline.

Tom rolled his eyes, “It’s a boating trip, Jerry. Who’s going to need their phone to charge? Besides, it’s one more thing that can fall overboard.”

“But it’s essential, Tom! What if there’s an emergency and we need to call for help?” Jerry’s voice rose with every word, his fear of being stranded on the river overtaking his common sense.

“Okay, okay,” Ellie interjected, attempting to mediate. “Let’s just decide on essentials. We’ll all have our phones, right? And probably won’t use them much.”

Tom nodded in agreement. “Right. Waterproof camera, first-aid kit, snacks, water, maps, oars...”

“Oars?!” Jerry’s tone turned incredulous. “You’re thinking we’ll row around all day? We’re on a motorboat!”

“Not all of us are trusting in the technology not to fail, Jerry,” Ellie said dryly. “And suppose the engine does cut out, or we hit a snag. It’s good to have a backup.”

Jerry muttered something under his breath, his hands fumbling to make sure the charger was indeed packed.

“And speaking of backups,” Tom continued, “sunscreen, hats, sunglasses, and, most importantly, a map of the Thames – just in case our GPS fritzes out.”

“GPS fritzes out?” Jerry snorted. “That thing has never failed me!”

“Oh, great,” Ellie chimed in. “Let’s just hope your faith in technology keeps us from running aground.”

The banter continued as they loaded the boat, each person stubbornly holding onto the items they deemed indispensable, until the boat was stuffed to capacity and the motor hummed to life, ready to carry them downstream. The disagreement was unresolved, but their laughter echoed across the water as they set off, each friend certain they had brought the most crucial thing for their adventure on the Thames.
