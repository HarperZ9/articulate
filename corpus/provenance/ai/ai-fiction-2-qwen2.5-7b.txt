The afternoon sun was casting long shadows over the calm waters of the Thames, and the smell of fresh hay was mingling with the scent of salt from the distant sea. Tom, Emma, and Jack sat on the weathered wooden benches of their small, rowdy barge, their faces framed by the low-hanging branches of the willows that lined the river. 

"I say we stick to the basics," Tom began, his voice clear and resolute. "Maps, compasses, waterproofs, food, and a first-aid kit. No fancy gadgets or gadgets at all."

Emma, with her chestnut curls cascading over her shoulders, rolled her eyes. "You're a born minimalist, Tom. But come on, what if we encounter a sudden storm? Have you seen the weather reports? They’re predicting everything from a sprinkle to a deluge. A tent and a couple of lightweight sleeping bags wouldn’t hurt."

Jack, who had been quietly mending a net, looked up, his hands still holding his tools. "But Emma, we’re not going to stay out in the wild. We’re on a barge. It’s like a floating house. Besides, we’re not going to need a tent when we can just park on the deck."

Tom shifted uncomfortably in his seat. "That’s all well and good, Jack, but what if the current takes us to the middle of nowhere? We’d be in the middle of a river, not a barge, and we’d need shelter."

Emma leaned closer, her voice low and persuasive. "Fine, fine, but at least add a couple of folding chairs and a deck of cards. It’s not a survival mission, it’s a holiday. We need to be prepared for the little comforts too."

Jack smiled, his eyes twinkling. "Little comforts, huh? What’s next, a bottle of wine and some chocolate?"

Tom chuckled, the tension easing between them. "Alright, alright. A couple of chairs and some cards. But no wine or chocolate. I need to keep my wits about me."

The friends laughed, the sound mixing with the gentle lapping of water against the side of the barge. They knew that the river, with its unpredictable weather and hidden dangers, could be both a threat and a friend. As they cast their lines into the water, their plans for the day began to take shape.
